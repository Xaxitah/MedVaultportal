## Estrutura do Mapa

**Genitourinary Trauma**
  - ### General Overview
    - 10% of abdominal traumas
    - Frequency: Rim > Ureter > Bexiga > Uretra > Genital
    - Key Formula: Mechanism + Exam + Conduct
  - ### Renal Trauma
    - Most common (1-5% of traumas)
    - Diagnosis: Flank pain, Hematuria, Palpable mass
    - Gold Standard: CT with contrast
    - AAST Classification
      - Grades I-III: Conservative treatment
      - Grade IV: Laceration/Vascular (Embolization/Surgery)
      - Grade V: Shattered kidney (Emergency surgery)
  - ### Ureteral Trauma
    - Rare in external trauma (<1%)
    - Main cause: Iatrogenic surgery
    - Diagnosis: CT excretory phase
    - Treatment: Stent DJ or Surgical repair
  - ### Bladder Trauma
    - Associated with pelvic fractures (70-90%)
    - Standard Exam: Retrograde cystogram
    - Types
      - Extraperitoneal: Foley catheter (10-14 days)
      - Intraperitoneal: Mandatory surgery
  - ### Urethral Trauma
    - Rule: Never catheterize before Retrograde Urethrography
    - Posterior Urethra
      - Mechanism: Pelvic fracture
      - Triad: Urethrorrhagia, High prostate, Urinary retention
    - Anterior Urethra
      - Mechanism: Straddle injury
      - Butterfly wings hematoma (Buck's fascia)
    - Urgent Conduct: Suprapubic cystostomy
  - ### Genital Trauma
    - Penile Fracture
      - Rupture of tunica albuginea
      - Eggplant appearance
      - Immediate surgery
    - Scrotal/Testicular
      - Exam: Ultrasound with Doppler
      - Surgery if albuginea is ruptured
