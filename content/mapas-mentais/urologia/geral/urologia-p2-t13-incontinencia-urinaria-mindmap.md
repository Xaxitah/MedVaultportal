## Estrutura do Mapa

**Urinary Incontinence (UI)**
  - ### Overview
    - Definition: Involuntary loss of urine
    - Epidemiology
      - More frequent in women (5-8x)
      - Increases with age (> 75 years)
      - High prevalence: 30-40% of adult women
  - ### Stress Urinary Incontinence (SUI)
    - Mechanism
      - Urethral hypermobility
      - Intrinsic sphincter deficiency
    - Triggers: Cough, sneeze, exercise, laughter
    - Risk Factors
      - Vaginal births (multiparity)
      - Obesity
      - Menopause (atrophy)
      - Cystocele
    - Diagnosis: Valsalva maneuver, Pad test, Urodynamics
    - Treatment
      - Kegel exercises (1st line)
      - Duloxetine
      - Sling (TVT/TOT) - Gold Standard
  - ### Urge Urinary Incontinence (UUI)
    - Mechanism: Detrusor overactivity
    - Overactive Bladder (OAB) Symptoms
      - Sudden urgency
      - Frequency (>8x/day)
      - Nocturia
    - Causes: Idiopathic, Neurological, Local irritation
    - Pharmacology
      - Antimuscarinics (Oxybutynin, Solifenacin)
      - Mirabegron (Preferred for elderly)
    - Invasive: Botox (detrusor), Sacral neuromodulation
  - ### Overflow Incontinence
    - Mechanism: Bladder overdistension
    - Clinical Signs
      - Continuous dripping
      - Palpable bladder (globus)
      - Post-void residual > 300mL
    - Causes: BPH (males), Cystocele (females), Diabetes
    - Treatment: Treat BPH, Intermittent catheterization
  - ### Mixed and Functional UI
    - Mixed: SUI + UUI (Common in >75 years)
    - Functional: Normal urinary tract, limited mobility
