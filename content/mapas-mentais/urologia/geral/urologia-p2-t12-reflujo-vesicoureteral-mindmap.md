## Estrutura do Mapa

**Vesicoureteral Reflux (VUR)**
  - ### Overview
    - Definition: Pathological return of urine to ureter/kidney
    - Classic Patient: Child 2-6 years with recurrent UTI
    - Main Risk: Permanent renal scarring/pyelonephritis
  - ### Pathophysiology
    - Mechanism: Short intramural ureter portion
    - Functional Sphincter: Detrusor compression failure
    - Ideal Ratio: 5:1 length to diameter
    - Ideal Length: 3-5 cm intramural segment
  - ### Etiology
    - Primary: Congenitally short intramural ureter (Most common)
    - Secondary: Neurogenic bladder (Mielomeningocele)
    - Associated: Eagle-Barrett (Prune Belly) Syndrome
    - Other: Ureterocele, Chronic Cystitis, Iatrogenic
  - ### Classification (Grades)
    - Grade I: Reaches ureter only
    - Grade II: Reaches kidney; no dilation
    - Grade III: Mild dilation of ureter/kidney
    - Grade IV: Marked dilation and tortuosity
    - Grade V: Severe ureteronephrosis; high pressure
  - ### Diagnosis
    - Gold Standard: Voiding Cystourethrography (VCUG)
    - Screening: Renal Ultrasound (Hydronephrosis)
    - Scarring: DMSA Scintigraphy
    - Lab: Urine culture to confirm UTI
  - ### Management
    - Conservative (Grades I-III)
      - Continuous antibiotic prophylaxis
      - Annual VCUG follow-up
      - High spontaneous resolution rate
    - Surgical (Grades IV-V)
      - Gregoir: Extravesical elongation
      - Cohen: Transtrigonal reimplantation
      - Politano-Leadbetter: Intravesical tunnel
      - Urgent: Urinary diversion for Grade V
